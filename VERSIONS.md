# Versions — Variant A: absurd CLIENT

Concept: a real-sounding profession (ghostwriter) whose client is
impossible (dead celebrities). The job stays genuine; the client is the
twist. One concept, iterated v1–v8; only the best version ships.

| Ver | Title | Chars | What changed and why |
|-----|-------|-------|----------------------|
| v1 | Ghostwriter for Dead Celebrities | 32 | Baseline concept: a real job (ghostwriter) + impossible clients (dead celebrities). Instant picture: someone taking dictation from a ghost. |
| v2 | Ghostwriter for Deceased Celebrities | 36 | Tried the formal register ("deceased" reads like intake paperwork). Deadpan, but "dead" hits harder on first read — reverted the word, kept the lesson. |
| v3 | Ghostwriter for Dead Pop Stars | 30 | Narrowed the client for specificity (pop stars have memoirs). More concrete, but loses the breadth of "celebrities" — every dead famous person counts. |
| v4 | Ghostwriter for Dead Rock Stars | 31 | Same narrowing, rock stars (posthumous rock memoirs are a real genre). Still narrower than v1; the client list should stay wide. |
| v5 | Celebrity Ghostwriter for the Dead | 34 | Reordered to front-load "Celebrity". "For the Dead" is cleaner, but it hides the client inside a vague phrase — "dead celebrities" names them directly. |
| v6 | Ghostwriter for the Dead and Famous | 35 | "Dead and famous" is snappier, but "famous" is vaguer than "celebrities" — a named client group beats an adjective. |
| v7 | Memoir Ghostwriter for Dead Celebrities | 39 | Added the deliverable: "Memoir" names the actual product, so you picture the ghost dictating their life story. Job stays real, client stays impossible. |
| v8 | Ghostwriter for Dead Celebrities' Memoirs | 41 | **SHIPPED.** Kept "Ghostwriter" as the job, kept the exact impossible client, and put the deliverable last so the twist lands in the final words: dead celebrities, dictating memoirs. 41 chars, reads instantly. |
